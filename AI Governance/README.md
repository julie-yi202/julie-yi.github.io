# **AI GRC/Governance Framework & Portfolio**

## **Overview**
This repository showcases a comprehensive, practical, and operational **AI GRC/Governance Framework** designed to help organizations adopt, manage, and monitor AI systems responsibly. It blends **GRC principles**, **technical controls**, and **regulatory alignment** to demonstrate how AI can be deployed safely, ethically, and securely.

The goal of this project is to provide:

- A complete governance model for AI systems  
- Templates and tools for risk assessments, model documentation, and compliance  
- Technical standards for secure and trustworthy AI development  
- Real‑world examples that demonstrate how governance is applied  

This repository reflects my professional experience in **Governance, Risk & Compliance (GRC)**, **risk management**, **privacy**, and **technical security workflows**, including DPIAs, control catalogs, and vulnerability scanning.

---

## **Why AI GRC/Governance Matters**
AI systems introduce unique risks — **bias**, **hallucinations**, **privacy violations**, **model drift**, **adversarial attacks**, and **regulatory exposure**. Organizations need structured governance to ensure AI systems are:

- **Safe**  
- **Fair**  
- **Transparent**  
- **Secure**  
- **Compliant**  
- **Accountable**  

This repository provides the artifacts needed to operationalize those principles.

# **Key Principles of This Framework**

### **1. Risk‑Based**
AI systems are evaluated based on their risk level, impact, and regulatory classification.

### **2. Lifecycle‑Oriented**
Governance spans the entire AI lifecycle:

- Design  
- Development  
- Testing  
- Deployment  
- Monitoring  
- Retirement  

### **3. Transparent**
Model cards, dataset cards, and documentation ensure clarity and accountability.

### **4. Secure**
Security controls address adversarial attacks, data leakage, and model manipulation.

### **5. Compliant**
Aligned with:

- EU AI Act  
- GDPR  
- ISO 42001  
- NIST AI RMF  
- OECD AI Principles  

### **6. Practical**
Templates and examples make governance actionable, not theoretical.
